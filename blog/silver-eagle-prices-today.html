The American Silver Eagle is the most popular silver bullion coin in the United States. Minted by the US Mint since 1986, it carries a $1 face value and contains exactly 1 troy ounce of .999 fine silver. But what you actually pay is the spot price plus a dealer premium — and that premium varies significantly from dealer to dealer. This page tracks today's best Silver Eagle prices.

Why Silver Eagle Premiums Fluctuate

US Mint allocation limits. The US Mint allocates Silver Eagles to authorized dealers. When demand is high and supply is constrained, premiums spike. In 2021–2022, Eagles routinely traded at $8–$12 over spot. As supply normalized in 2023–2024, premiums fell back to historic norms of $3–$6.

Spot price volatility. When silver spot moves sharply, premiums can temporarily expand as dealers hedge inventory risk. Major moves of $1+ in a single day often cause brief premium spikes.

Type 1 vs Type 2. In 2021, the US Mint redesigned the reverse of the Silver Eagle (Type 2). Both are genuine .999 fine silver, but Type 1 coins (pre-2021) sometimes trade at a slight discount to Type 2 from certain dealers.

Tips for Buying Silver Eagles at the Best Price

Buy in quantities of 20. Silver Eagles come in tubes of 20 coins. Buying a full tube often qualifies for lower premiums at most dealers and reduces per-coin shipping cost.

Compare total cost including shipping. A deal that's $0.50/oz cheaper might cost more after shipping if you don't hit the free shipping threshold.

Watch for sales. Dealers frequently run "Flash Sales" or weekend specials with reduced premiums. Sign up for dealer email alerts.

Pay with check or ACH. Most dealers charge 3–4% extra for credit card payments. Paying by check or bank transfer saves more than the difference between most dealer prices.
